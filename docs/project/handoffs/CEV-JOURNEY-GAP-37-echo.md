## Guided setup gaps

Business owners currently cannot complete integrations through the application. Settings cover only business name, trade and timezone. Define tenant-owned services/service area, hours/holidays, booking rules, escalation contacts and approved caller information; persist them through verified owner/admin workflows. Define granular progress independently of provider readiness. No secret, URL or environment-file entry should be required in ordinary client flows; secure connection management and any platform operator provisioning need separately reviewed implementation.

Integration cards need backend-derived states such as not configured, setup incomplete, awaiting verification, verified but disabled, disconnected/revoked and failed. Labels are proposed, not accepted contracts. Provide a reason, last checked time and next permitted action without exposing keys/private endpoints/account IDs. Never show connected because a provider is selected, a key exists or a local test passed. Disable/deny activation server-side until approved gates pass; disabled/disconnected is distinct from no call activity. Unknown/unavailable activity must not render a fabricated zero metric.

Proposed sequence: save business facts and policy → select already-approved integration path → privately establish tenant-owned connection → validate safe prerequisites → show blocking evidence → review/authorize activation only under separate scope. Ordinary onboarding should remain useful and resumable while integrations are blocked, with helpful retry/permission errors. Saving setup, completing onboarding and enabling live calls are distinct outcomes.

Consent and escalation need specific contracts: notification purpose/channel, recorded consent provenance/revocation, destination ownership and opt-out handling; eligible staff/roles, hours/timezone, acknowledgement deadline, no-answer escalation and resolution authority. Model-inferred Yes/No fields alone are not consent evidence. Do not select handoff timeouts, emergency advice or recipients without project authority. Transfer completion is not human resolution; provider message acceptance is not delivery.
