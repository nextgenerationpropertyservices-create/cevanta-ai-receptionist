## Minimal model candidates

**Estimate aggregate:** tenant-owned UUID identity, tenant-scoped creation token, customer reference, optional job/lead/location references subject to the approved association rule, bounded title/scope text, draft status, optimistic revision and server-generated actor/timestamps. Recommend a known customer as the minimum recipient anchor; owner must decide whether a lead-only estimate is required before customer conversion. Do not automatically convert a lead, create a job, change its status or reserve an appointment upon estimate creation/acceptance.

**Line items:** separate tenant-owned child rows with estimate/revision reference, stable identity, deterministic ordering, bounded description and optional validated equipment reference. After commercial decisions, add explicit quantity/unit and exact monetary values under one declared estimate currency. No implicit unit, quantity1, zero-price default, tax-free flag, price catalog or cost/margin field should enter the scope by assumption. Use exact decimal arithmetic or defined integer monetary units, not binary floating point; representation/scale/limits and rounding must follow the approved currency and quantity rules. Money in shared JSON contracts should serialize as exact strings or a specified safe integer representation. Totals are server-derived from the approved rules, not accepted from browser fields.

**Manual follow-up:** start with an estimate-linked due date, bounded internal note and completion metadata if one reminder per estimate meets the owner's workflow. Only introduce a child follow-up table if multiple tasks/history/assignees are required. Candidate fields then include tenant/estimate identity, task UUID/creation token, due date, optional authorized member assignee, open/completed/cancelled state and server completion actor/time. Never silently reuse or overwrite the lead's reminder. A date-only office reminder and an exact notification instant are different contracts: do not infer automatic execution time or timezone from a due date.

**Audit/revision history:** atomically record identifiers, actor, operation and time with estimate/line-item/follow-up writes, without copying customer details, line descriptions, legal terms or full payloads into audit logs. If external delivery or acceptance is later approved, preserve the immutable content revision actually delivered/reviewed; future customer/location/equipment edits must not silently change that revision. Structured private estimate content is not an audit payload. Decide snapshot/access/retention and cancellation rather than implementing destructive cascades.

## Tenant and relationship invariants

- Every estimate, revision, line and follow-up row retains tenant ownership; `(tenant_id, id)` composite targets and child foreign keys prevent cross-business references. Ordinary requests cannot rewrite IDs, tenant ownership, generated timestamps, creation tokens or parent identities through broad UPDATE grants.
- A same-tenant foreign key alone does not prove the correct customer chain. If job and customer are both present, require job.customer_id to agree with the estimate customer; a job with no customer requires an explicit supported workflow, not silent customer assignment. Optional lead linkage must agree with its known customer and, where linked, the job's originating lead; explicitly define whether an independently sourced job/lead combination is permitted.
- Location must belong to that customer using the existing `(tenant_id, customer_id, location_id)` relationship. Equipment must belong to the linked service location; current equipment lacks a composite unique target suitable for the full chain, so later migration must introduce/review the necessary target or equivalent transactional constraint. Do not permit equipment-only linkage to bypass customer/location checks.
- Parent links and line references must be rechecked atomically when accepted and when revisions change. Existing lead.customer_id can change, so a one-time application check cannot establish a permanent cross-record invariant. Prefer immutable origin references plus explicit captured-context semantics, or enforce live consistency across every affected parent mutation under a separate ownership assignment; do not silently change existing CRM/intake behavior.
- Index tenant/parent joins and approved list access patterns: estimate customer/job, line estimate/revision, follow-up estimate/due date/status. Do not declare one-estimate-per-job or one-per-lead uniqueness: repeated proposals/revisions may be legitimate, and cardinality is an owner decision. Tenant-scoped retry uniqueness does not restrict legitimate business cardinality.

## Lifecycle and commands — candidates only

The smallest internal-only workflow could be `draft → cancelled` with draft editing and manual follow-up; this is not a full estimate acceptance flow. If broader lifecycle is approved, candidate states are draft, issued, accepted, declined, cancelled and superseded; expired is needed only if validity/expiry policy is agreed. Sent/delivered describes an evidenced delivery attempt, not content readiness. Viewed is not accepted; manual office recording is not customer-authenticated acceptance. No legal or e-signature meaning attaches to proposed enum labels.

Before implementation, specify permitted transitions, actors, evidence, reversibility, financial lock point and revision behavior. Editing an issued/accepted revision should normally create a new draft revision rather than alter historical content, subject to the approved workflow. Recording acceptance does not imply payment, job completion or recovered revenue.

Potential command contracts: create draft with tenant-scoped retry token; edit draft atomically with expected revision; explicit lifecycle transition with expected revision and evidence permitted by that workflow; set/complete/cancel manual follow-up. Verify current user, tenant membership and operation role before fetching parents/mutating; derive actor/tenant context server-side, validate any selected workspace and related records. Responses distinguish confirmed save, validation failure, denied/unavailable record, revision conflict and retryable failure. Do not return success until aggregate/audit transaction commits. Cross-tenant errors expose no foreign customer or estimate details.

Retry keys must replay an original committed result without replacing changed payloads; conflicting key reuse must reject. Header plus complete line changes, derived totals and audit must commit as one transaction. A completed follow-up stays completed on retries without duplicate audit effects. If future delivery is authorized, use a durable outbox and attempt/operation identity independent of estimate ID; unknown provider outcome requires reconciliation. None of that authorizes a scheduler or messages in M5's current scope.

## Authorization candidates and decisions

Existing office roles maintain CRM/jobs, but that does not automatically authorize commercial approval or price visibility. Recommend owner/admin draft management, and dispatcher draft management only after owner confirms pricing authority. Technician and viewer access to prices/terms is unresolved; do not inherit all-CRM visibility. If technician estimate read is approved, it must follow current assigned job visibility and active membership, not a copied assignment or customer association; unlinked estimates remain inaccessible to technicians unless a separately approved rule exists. Specify viewer/office read privileges explicitly. Follow-up assignee membership never alone grants access to its estimate.

RLS and explicit column/table grants apply to header, items, revisions and follow-up; child reads/writes follow parent authorization. Deny anonymous access and unauthorized delete. Revoke defaults explicitly before intentional grants. Protected workflow changes cannot be circumvented by direct API line/price/status writes; choose transaction commands and grants that enforce lifecycle invariants in the database as well as the server. Exposed views/RPCs must preserve invoker isolation or have tightly reviewed privilege boundaries. Ordinary app requests must not use service-role access. See [Supabase RLS guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security).

2. Operating currency and whether currency is tenant-wide or selectable per estimate; quantity/unit rules, price precision, rounding and permitted zero/negative values; no country, currency or default prices assumed.
3. Tax treatment, whether entered prices include/exclude tax, exemptions/multiple rates and how authoritative rules are supplied; discounts, validity and commercial/legal terms only if explicitly requested. No tax calculation or tax-free default before decisions.
4. Who can read prices, create/edit drafts, issue, cancel and record acceptance; evidence of manual versus customer-authenticated acceptance and revision locking.
5. Delivery channel/recipient verification and consent requirements if delivery is in scope. Automated messaging, reminder schedules, opt-outs, retries and provider approval are separate choices. Payment, financing and e-signature are not implied.
6. Manual reminder cardinality/assignee and due-date meaning; retention and authorized deletion/cancellation for historical estimate/acceptance evidence.
